The Problem

A crack in the sensor can look “cosmetic only”—until the area is exposed. Dirt, sweat and splashes enter through the fissure; optical reading becomes unstable; the watch is less reliable day to day. Without protection, the crack can get worse.

Exposed area

With cracked glass, protection against dirt and splashes around the sensor is compromised.

Vulnerable sensor

The crack leaves the optical sensor more exposed to impact, sweat and dirt.

Unstable reading

The crack interferes with the optical interface—heart-rate reading may fail.

Compromised sealing

With cracked glass, sealing at the sensor fails: water, sweat and moisture become a risk until the area is properly covered.

Expensive repair

Official module/sensor replacement is costly and slow—many prefer an immediate cover.

Risk of getting worse

Without protection, the crack can grow—and sensor damage gets worse.

Why quick fixes fail

After a crack, many people improvise—and make it worse:

  • Tape and generic stickers: They peel with sweat; cover poorly and dirty the sensor.
  • DIY glue / resin: Can push moisture inside, leave residue and make a future repair harder.
  • Ignoring the crack: Damage tends to grow and optical reading gets worse with use.

How to apply

Step by step from the kit manual. Follow the order—cleaning matters as much as alignment.

  1. Clean the sensor with the kit wipe until oil and dust are gone.
  2. Peel the film from the lens adhesive.
  3. Align the lens on the sensor center (diameter sized for your model).
  4. Press (firmly, but without further damaging the sensor) for about 30 seconds with the applicator, without edge bubbles.

When NOT to use the kit

CrashFix covers the sensor glass. In these cases, seek service—the kit does not replace structural repair.

  • Module broken beyond the glass—damaged internals or a displaced sensor.
  • Loose parts—back cover, screws or rattling components.
  • Moisture already inside the watch—internal fogging, failures after getting wet, corrosion.
